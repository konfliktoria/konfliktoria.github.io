As a lot of you have probably heard, there are lots of wildfires going on in Canada. I think the one in Ontario received the most coverage in European news. A weeks ago, I honestly can’t even remember exactly when a few wildfires started in the area. Some fires started quite close to Mount Bowmen, which Margot, Vanessa and I hiked at the beginning of July. They always felt quite far away until they reached Clinton. Clinton is about an hour away from the ranch by car. We have been there, had some ice cream and visited a cute vintage shop. Also the fire jumped the highway to the other side and is burning close to the Chasm, which we also visited a few weeks back. 

On Friday, almost one week ago, the day started as usual. I was working on my own, because Savana was off. During the ride the atmosphere was already a bit eerie, the light was kind of yellowish, you could hear no bird sounds or just a few at some points. When we came back and were done untacking the horses, the sky started to look worse and worse. There were some dark clouds and lots of smoke coming in. It started getting really dark at around 5:15pm and I even had to turn on the light in the barn. It got smokier by the minute and at one point it started smelling as if you were standing right next to a camp fire. The smoke started stinging in my eyes and I just finished up with my chores and sent the horses into the home meadow. I went to staff house, because the air quality was getting worse and worse and we were not supposed to stay in our tents. At 7.30pm when it is usually still bright and sunny outside, the smoke and ash were so thick, that we had a complete blackout. It was darker than at night. Whenever the smoke shifted you could see a very orange/red sky. It was very scary and a very apocalyptic atmosphere. 
Everybody had to sleep inside, so I decided to go with Cam and Margot to the Cabin. When we got there, we had no power - probably because there is a big power plant close to Clinton that they might have shut down because to the fire. It was quite hard to fall asleep but as we had no evacuation order and the fire was still kind of far away and wouldn’t reach us, there was nothing to do anyway. The next morning it had cleared up a lot and the sun was shining. But everything was covered in ashes. The whole evening felt very surreal. Even some of the staff members who have experienced bad smoke said that it was one of the worst they have ever seen. 

Since then we have been quite lucky and it hasn’t been that smoky again. It still gets smoky almost every day, depending on the wind though. The sun turns very orange or red in the smoke, which looks kind of cool but also scary, if that happens at 3pm. Unfortunately the wildfires are still growing and the little town of 70 Mile is also being evacuated. It’s the closest town with a general store and we always pass it on our way to go grocery shopping (and buying donuts) at 100 Mile House. Today the evacuation order expanded even further, all the way to Green Lake. A friend of Margot is working as a firefighter for this exact fire, which is very good, because he can give us even more information than what you can find online. The likelihood that the fire will reach the ranch is not very high, but it is still scary and it will be devastating to see the burned areas, once the fire went out and we can drive that way again. But it will probably still take a while and the fires are still spreading rapidly. 

It’s one of those things that you always hear in the news and think it won’t be where you live, but it is very real right now. Every day we have to see if we put the horses on the range or in the home meadow. Every evening I have to decide if it is too smoky to work out or if it is still fine to do so. It sucks, that there is nothing we can do. We just try to carry on with business as usual and monitor the wildfires. 

Living and working outdoors in such remote areas this year has taught me a lot. And seeing what climate change does, makes me become even more aware of that. Almost too much rain in Zimbabwe, drought and wildfires in Canada. I feel like in the city it is way easier to forget about it. Yes, you get major heat waves in Europe and they do influence you. But it is still different when you just go and work in your office or if you still have to work outdoors and cope with the weather conditions. Experiencing all this honestly makes me appreciate the good times and everything that I have seen even more. I feel very sorry for all the people who lost their homes and businesses and to everyone who really is in fear of losing it. For now I am safe where I am and very grateful for that. All we can do is hope for rain, keep an eye on the fires, and take one day at a time.
